Borehole Solarisation Cost in Kenya (2026 Guide)

Are you considering solar power for your borehole? A solar water pumping system can provide an alternative to relying entirely on grid electricity or a fuel-powered generator. The right system, however, depends on more than borehole depth or pump horsepower.

Before estimating the cost of borehole solarisation in Kenya, it is important to understand the water requirement, pumping conditions, equipment specifications and installation work involved. This guide explains these factors and the information needed to plan a suitable system.

What is borehole solarisation?

Borehole solarisation is the use of electricity generated by solar panels to operate a water pump. A compatible solar pump controller regulates the electrical supply and controls the pump.

The pump lifts water from the borehole and delivers it to a storage tank, irrigation system or another point of use. Depending on the design, the system may pump water during daylight, with stored water available for use later.

Solar pumping can be considered for homes, farms, livestock operations, institutions and commercial properties where a water supply is required.

What determines borehole solarisation cost in Kenya?

There is no single price that applies to every borehole. The total cost depends on the pump, solar equipment, site conditions and installation requirements.

1. Borehole water level and pumping depth

The total drilled depth does not, by itself, determine the pump size. The static water level and dynamic water level during pumping help establish how far the pump must lift the water.

The borehole's water-level behaviour should be assessed alongside its yield and the intended pumping rate. This helps avoid selecting a pump that is unsuitable for the available water supply.

2. Total dynamic head

Total dynamic head is the overall head the pump must overcome while delivering water. It includes the vertical lift from the pumping water level to the delivery point, the pressure required at the outlet and friction losses through pipes, bends, valves and fittings.

Accurate head calculations are essential. A pump may have an adequate power rating but still fail to deliver the required flow if it is not selected for the actual operating head.

3. Daily water demand

The system should be designed around the amount of water required each day and the time available for pumping.

A household, livestock farm, irrigation project and commercial property may have very different water requirements. The design should consider daily volume, peak demand, pumping hours and the borehole's sustainable yield.

4. Pump type and specifications

The selected pump must be suitable for the borehole, water conditions, required flow rate and calculated total dynamic head.

Its performance curve should be checked to establish the expected water delivery at the intended operating point. Selecting equipment based only on horsepower or advertised maximum flow can lead to an unsuitable installation.

5. Solar panels and pump controller

The solar array must supply the electrical energy required by the pump under the expected site conditions. Panel quantity and capacity depend on pump specifications, operating hours, solar availability, controller limits and system losses.

The controller must be compatible with the pump motor and the selected solar array. These components should be designed as one system rather than purchased independently without checking compatibility.

6. Pipes, cables and electrical protection

Pipe diameter, length and fittings affect friction losses and water delivery. Electrical cable sizing must account for current, cable distance and acceptable voltage drop.

The installation may also require isolators, protective devices, suitable earthing, connectors, mounting materials and environmental protection for electrical equipment.

7. Water storage and distribution

A water storage tank can help separate the pumping schedule from the time water is used. Water can be pumped when solar energy is available and stored for later household, livestock or irrigation needs.

Tank capacity, elevation, pipework, outlet pressure and any additional booster pump should be considered when planning the complete water supply system.

How to size a solar borehole pumping system

Correct sizing begins with the water requirement and borehole information, not with an arbitrary panel count or pump rating.

A professional assessment should establish:

  • Borehole depth and available completion records.
  • Static and dynamic water levels, where measured.
  • Borehole yield and recommended pumping limits.
  • Required daily water volume.
  • Preferred pumping schedule and delivery time.
  • Height of the delivery point or storage tank.
  • Required outlet pressure, where applicable.
  • Pipe material, diameter, length and route.
  • Water quality and possible sediment concerns.
  • Available space and shading conditions for solar panels.
  • Whether an alternative power source or backup arrangement is required.

Why the pump performance curve matters

A pump's power rating alone does not tell you how much water it will deliver at a particular height. Flow rate changes with pumping head, and the manufacturer's performance curve shows the expected operating range.

The pump should be selected to deliver the required flow at the calculated total dynamic head while respecting the borehole's sustainable yield.

The solar array and controller must then be matched to the selected pump and the site's solar conditions. Incorrectly matched components can affect water output, operating reliability and overall project cost.

Solar pumping compared with grid and generator pumping

Solar pumping uses energy generated by solar panels. Grid-powered pumping depends on the electricity supply and applicable electricity tariffs, while generator-powered pumping depends on fuel availability, fuel costs and generator maintenance.

The appropriate arrangement depends on the site's existing infrastructure, water demand, pumping schedule and the cost of operating the available alternatives.

Solar pumping does not remove every operating expense. Panels require cleaning and inspection, while pumps, controllers, pipework and electrical connections may need servicing or repair.

Do solar borehole pumps need batteries?

Many solar water-pumping systems can operate during daylight without a battery bank. Instead of storing electricity, the system pumps water into a storage tank for use when required.

Battery storage may be considered where the operating requirements call for electrical energy outside available solar hours. However, batteries add equipment, cost and maintenance considerations.

The decision should be based on the required pumping schedule, water storage arrangements and overall system design.

Installation and maintenance considerations

Good installation practice helps ensure that the selected equipment operates within its intended conditions. Important considerations include:

  • Reviewing borehole records and confirming the pumping requirements.
  • Checking pump specifications and installation depth.
  • Using suitable pipework, cables and electrical protection.
  • Installing panels on a stable structure with suitable orientation and minimal shading.
  • Protecting the controller and electrical connections from water and environmental exposure.
  • Inspecting panel surfaces, mounting structures, cables and connections periodically.
  • Investigating changes in water flow, unusual noise, repeated electrical trips or excessive sediment.

Why a site assessment is important

Two boreholes with similar depths may require different pumping systems. Their water levels, yields, delivery heights, pipework and daily demand may not be the same.

A site assessment helps establish the actual requirements before equipment is selected. It also provides a better basis for preparing a quotation that reflects the work and components required at the property.

Frequently asked questions

Can solar panels run a borehole pump?

Yes. A suitable solar array and compatible pump controller can supply the electrical power needed to operate a borehole pump. The equipment must be selected according to the pump's electrical requirements and the required water output.

How many solar panels are needed for a borehole pump?

The number depends on the pump specifications, required water volume, total dynamic head, panel rating, controller limits and available solar energy. It should be calculated as part of the complete system design.

Can a solar borehole pump supply water for irrigation?

Yes, provided the borehole yield, pump performance, daily water requirement, storage and irrigation distribution system are properly assessed. Irrigation demand depends on factors such as the crop, cultivated area and watering method.

Will a solar borehole pump work on cloudy days?

Reduced sunlight lowers the energy available from the solar array and may reduce pumping performance. The design should account for local solar conditions and consider water storage or an alternative backup arrangement where necessary.

Is solar borehole pumping cheaper than generator pumping?

The answer depends on the existing power source, fuel or electricity costs, water demand, equipment requirements, installation cost and maintenance. A comparison should use the actual operating conditions of the site rather than assume a fixed percentage of savings.

Does every borehole need the same pump size?

No. Pump selection depends on water levels, borehole yield, total dynamic head, required flow rate and the intended application. Similar borehole depths do not necessarily mean identical pumping requirements.
