Why solar system maintenance matters

A solar power system contains several components that work together, including panels, mounting structures, cables, protection devices, an inverter, batteries where installed and the connected electrical loads.

Periodic inspection can help identify visible damage, loose connections, abnormal operating readings and other issues that may affect system operation. Maintenance needs depend on the equipment, installation conditions, environment and how the system is used.

What solar system maintenance can include

  • System inspection: Checking accessible equipment, mounting structures, visible wiring and general installation condition.
  • Solar panel cleaning: Removing accumulated dust and dirt using suitable cleaning methods recommended for the panel.
  • Inverter checks: Reviewing display readings, operating status, warning messages and reported faults.
  • Battery checks: Reviewing battery status, settings, connections and available monitoring information where batteries are installed.
  • Charge controller checks: Inspecting controller status and settings in systems that use a separate charge controller.
  • Electrical inspection: Checking accessible cables, connectors, isolators and protective devices for signs of damage or deterioration.
  • Performance review: Comparing available system readings and reported energy production with the system's expected operation.

The actual work required should be determined from the system type, equipment condition and findings during assessment.

Common solar system problems

If a solar installation is not operating as expected, the cause should be investigated rather than assuming that a particular component has failed.

  • Solar panels appear not to be charging the batteries.
  • The inverter displays an error or warning code.
  • Solar production appears lower than expected.
  • The battery discharges sooner than usual.
  • The system switches off or changes to backup power unexpectedly.
  • A circuit breaker or protective device trips repeatedly.
  • Cables, connectors or electrical accessories show visible damage.

Possible causes include shading, dirt, equipment settings, battery condition, wiring faults, protection devices or changes in electrical loads. Accurate diagnosis requires the relevant system information and appropriate checks.

Solar panel cleaning and inspection

Dust, leaves and other deposits can accumulate on solar panels and may reduce the sunlight reaching the cells. The effect depends on the amount and type of soiling, the panel installation and local conditions.

Panels should be inspected and cleaned when necessary using methods suitable for the manufacturer's instructions. Avoid abrasive materials, harsh chemicals and unsafe access methods. Where panels are installed at height or electrical equipment may be exposed, use an appropriately competent person.

Inverter and battery maintenance

Inverters and batteries should be checked according to their manufacturers' instructions. Relevant information may include inverter warning codes, operating settings, battery status, charging behaviour and visible condition of cables and connections.

Battery replacement should not be assumed to be the solution whenever a system has reduced backup time. The battery, charging settings, connected loads, wiring and inverter operation may all need to be assessed before a replacement is recommended.

Different battery chemistries and models have different operating and maintenance requirements. Follow the instructions for the equipment installed and do not open or attempt to repair sealed battery units.

When to arrange a solar system inspection

Consider arranging an assessment if you notice repeated inverter warnings, unusual system behaviour, reduced backup time, damaged cables, visible panel damage or a change in energy production that cannot be explained by weather or usage.

If you notice burning smells, smoke, sparking, overheating or exposed electrical conductors, keep people away from the affected equipment. Do not touch damaged components or attempt live electrical repairs. Arrange for the affected supply to be isolated safely by a competent person.

Information to prepare before requesting maintenance

Providing basic system information can help explain the problem and identify what may need to be checked. Where possible, share:

  • Your name and location.
  • The type of property and how the solar system is used.
  • Inverter make, model and rated capacity, if known.
  • Battery type, make and capacity, if installed.
  • Solar panel details, if available.
  • The warning or fault code shown on the inverter.
  • When the problem started and what happens when it occurs.
  • Clear photos of equipment labels and display readings, where safe to take them.

Frequently asked questions

How often should solar panels be cleaned?

Cleaning frequency depends on local dust, rainfall, nearby trees, bird activity and the amount of soiling. Inspect panels periodically and clean them when deposits are present and cleaning can be carried out safely.

Why is my solar system not charging the battery?

Possible causes include insufficient solar input, shading, dirty panels, wiring or protection issues, inverter or controller settings, battery condition and equipment faults. The system should be checked before replacing components.

Why does my inverter display a fault code?

A fault code indicates that the inverter has detected a condition requiring attention. The meaning depends on the inverter make and model. Record the exact code and consult the manufacturer's documentation or a competent technician.

Does a solar battery need maintenance?

Maintenance requirements depend on the battery chemistry and model. Follow the manufacturer's guidance for inspection, charging settings, ventilation where applicable and safe operation. Do not open sealed batteries.

Should I replace my battery if backup time has reduced?

Not necessarily. Reduced backup time may result from battery ageing, increased electricity use, charging problems, incorrect settings or other system faults. Assessment can help identify the cause before a replacement is considered.

Can a solar system be repaired if it stops working?

Many reported problems can be investigated, but the appropriate repair depends on the fault, equipment condition and availability of compatible parts. Provide the inverter model, displayed error and a description of the symptoms when making an enquiry.
