Solar Installation Cost in Kenya (2026 Guide)

Planning a solar installation in Kenya starts with understanding your electricity needs, the type of system you require and the equipment needed to supply power. There is no single price that applies to every property. A small backup system for essential appliances is different from a larger installation for a home, business or farm.

This guide explains the main factors that influence installation cost, the equipment involved and the information to prepare before requesting a quotation.

What determines solar installation cost in Kenya?

1. Your electricity consumption

The first step is to identify the appliances you want to operate, their power ratings and how many hours they run each day. Daily energy consumption, measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh), helps determine the required solar panel capacity and battery storage.

For example, an appliance rated at 100 watts and used for five hours consumes 500 watt-hours, or 0.5 kWh, before system losses. Add the energy use of the appliances you intend to run to estimate your daily requirement.

Also consider appliances with high starting currents, such as refrigerators, water pumps and some motors. Their starting demand can affect inverter selection even when their daily energy consumption is modest.

2. The type of solar system

  • Grid-tied: Works alongside the electricity grid. Battery storage is not normally included. A standard grid-tied inverter generally disconnects during a grid outage unless the installation has specifically designed backup equipment.
  • Hybrid: Combines solar panels, an inverter, battery storage and grid supply. It can provide backup during outages when the equipment and system are configured appropriately.
  • Off-grid: Designed to operate independently of the utility grid. Its design must account for energy demand, battery autonomy, available sunlight and periods of reduced solar generation.

Equipment and installation requirements differ between these configurations. Compare quotations for the same system type, intended use and backup requirements.

3. Solar panels and mounting

The number and rating of panels depend on the energy requirement, local solar conditions, system losses and the time available to recharge batteries. Roof condition, orientation, shading and mounting structure can also affect the installation work.

A proposal should state the panel quantity, individual panel wattage and total installed solar capacity. It should also explain any limitations caused by available roof or ground space.

4. Inverter capacity and features

The inverter must support the expected running load and the starting demand of relevant appliances. Other considerations include battery compatibility, phase requirements, charging capability, solar input limits, monitoring features and provision for future expansion.

Check the inverter's rated output and surge capability against the appliances that may operate at the same time. Do not select an inverter using total daily energy consumption alone.

5. Battery capacity and chemistry

Battery requirements depend on the appliances that must remain powered when solar generation is unavailable, the required backup duration and the permitted depth of discharge. Lithium and lead-acid batteries differ in cost, usable capacity, maintenance requirements and operating characteristics.

Compare usable energy, battery compatibility and warranty conditions rather than comparing purchase prices or nominal kWh figures alone. The proposed design should explain the expected loads and the assumptions used to estimate backup time.

6. Protection, wiring and installation work

A complete quotation should account for suitable DC and AC protection, isolators, breakers, surge protection where appropriate, correctly sized cables, earthing, connectors, mounting materials and installation labour. Site access, roof height, cable distances and electrical upgrades may affect the work involved.

Ask the installer to identify included equipment and any exclusions or additional work that may be required after inspecting the property.

Typical equipment in a solar installation

Solar panels

Convert sunlight into DC electricity for the system.

Solar inverter

Converts DC electricity into usable AC power and manages system operation according to its design.

Battery storage

Stores energy for use when solar generation is insufficient or unavailable, where storage is part of the design.

Electrical protection

Helps protect equipment and wiring when correctly selected, installed and maintained.

How to estimate the size of your solar system

  1. List the appliances you want to power.
  2. Record each appliance's wattage and expected daily operating hours.
  3. Calculate the daily energy requirement in kWh.
  4. Identify appliances that may run simultaneously and their starting demand.
  5. Choose the required backup duration and battery autonomy, if storage is needed.
  6. Allow for system losses, local sunlight conditions and appropriate design margins.
  7. Have the final inverter, panel and battery selection checked against actual site conditions.

Questions to ask before accepting a quotation

  • Does the quotation state the inverter make, model and rated capacity?
  • Does it list the panel quantity, wattage and manufacturer?
  • Does it state the battery chemistry, nominal capacity and usable capacity?
  • Are mounting, cabling, protection, earthing and labour included?
  • Are equipment warranties and workmanship terms clearly stated?
  • Does the design explain expected backup performance and its assumptions?
  • Are exclusions, optional items or additional site works identified?
  • Does the proposal explain whether the system can accommodate likely future loads?

Why a site assessment matters

A suitable design considers more than the appliance list. Roof condition, shading, cable routes, existing electrical installations, equipment location, ventilation and future expansion can all influence the final specification. A site assessment helps identify these requirements before equipment is selected.

It also gives the installer an opportunity to check the proposed equipment locations, cable routes, mounting arrangements and any changes needed to the existing electrical installation.

Frequently asked questions

How much does solar installation cost in Kenya?

The cost depends on system type, daily energy use, inverter capacity, panel quantity, battery storage, installation conditions and equipment specifications. A quotation based on your actual requirements is more useful than a generic package price.

Do I need batteries for my solar system?

Not every system requires batteries. Grid-tied systems can operate without battery storage, while hybrid and off-grid systems commonly include batteries when backup or energy storage is required.

Will a grid-tied solar system work during a power outage?

A standard grid-tied inverter normally disconnects during a grid outage for safety. Backup during an outage requires equipment and a system configuration specifically designed to provide it.

Can I expand my solar system later?

Expansion may be possible, but it depends on inverter capacity, battery compatibility, solar input limits, available installation space, protection equipment and the original system design. Discuss likely future loads before installation.

How long will a solar battery provide backup?

Backup time depends on usable battery energy and the total load being supplied. Higher loads use stored energy more quickly. A system proposal should state the assumed loads and intended backup duration.

Does a solar installation eliminate my electricity bill?

Not necessarily. The result depends on system configuration, energy consumption, solar production, grid usage and applicable utility arrangements. No fixed bill reduction should be assumed without an assessment of the property's usage and system design.

Can I install solar panels first and add batteries later?

That may be possible if the inverter and system architecture support the intended battery addition. Compatibility, inverter settings, protection equipment and available capacity should be checked before choosing a system on the assumption that it can be expanded.
