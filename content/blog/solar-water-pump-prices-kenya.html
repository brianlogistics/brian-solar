What to consider before choosing a solar water pumping system

Solar water pumping can provide a practical way to move water for homes, farms, livestock and institutions. A solar pumping system uses electricity generated by solar panels to operate a pump, usually through a suitable pump controller.

The cost of a complete installation varies considerably. The water source, pumping depth, required flow rate, pipework, site conditions and daily water demand all affect the equipment and installation work required. A pump price on its own is therefore not a reliable estimate of the cost of a complete system.

How Much Does a Solar Water Pump Cost in Kenya?

There is no single price that applies to every solar water pumping project. A small surface-water application and a deep borehole installation have different technical requirements. Commercial and agricultural projects may also need larger storage, distribution pipework and additional controls.

A useful quotation should identify the pump, controller, solar panels, mounting equipment, cables, protection devices, pipework and any other work included. It should also state what is excluded. Ask for an itemised quotation so that you can compare systems on the same basis.

Types of Solar Water Pumps

1. Surface Solar Pumps

Surface pumps are installed outside the water source and draw water from places such as tanks, ponds, rivers or suitable shallow wells. The pump must be appropriate for the suction conditions and the distance between the water surface and the pump. Not every surface pump is suitable for every source.

2. Submersible Solar Pumps

Submersible pumps operate underwater and are commonly used in boreholes and wells. Selection depends on the borehole diameter, pumping water level, required flow and total head. The pump must be matched to the borehole conditions and its performance curve.

3. Solar Pumps for Irrigation

Irrigation systems may supply water to drip lines, sprinklers, storage tanks or other distribution arrangements. The pump must deliver the flow and pressure required by the irrigation method. Crop water needs, irrigation schedules and the available water source should be considered before selecting equipment.

Water Level and Pumping Depth

For a borehole, the depth of the borehole is not necessarily the same as the depth from which the pump must lift water. The static water level is the level when the borehole is not being pumped. The pumping or dynamic water level is the level while water is being drawn. Drawdown is the difference between those levels.

The pumping water level is important when calculating the head the pump must overcome. Where available, use reliable borehole records or a properly conducted pumping test rather than estimating the water level from the total borehole depth.

Total Dynamic Head (TDH)

Total dynamic head is the overall resistance the pump must overcome to deliver water to the required outlet. It includes the vertical lift, the height of the delivery point, pressure requirements and friction losses through pipes, fittings and valves.

A longer or narrower pipe can increase friction losses. The pipe route, diameter, material, fittings and required delivery pressure should therefore be considered during system design.

Daily Water Demand and Flow Rate

The system should be selected around the quantity of water needed per day and when that water is required. A household, livestock unit and irrigated farm may have very different demand patterns. The required flow rate must also be achievable at the calculated total dynamic head.

Pump and Controller Selection

The pump performance curve helps show the flow a particular pump can deliver at different heads. Check the manufacturer's specifications to confirm that the selected pump can meet the required duty point. The controller must be compatible with the pump and the solar array.

Solar Panel Capacity

The solar array must be matched to the pump and controller specifications, expected operating conditions and daily water requirement. The number and wattage of panels should not be selected using pump power alone. Panel arrangement, cable distance, shading and the controller's permitted input range also matter.

Storage and Distribution

A storage tank can help make water available when the pump is not running. Its capacity should reflect daily demand, the pumping schedule and the available water source. The delivery system may also require distribution pipes, valves, float switches or other controls.

Installation Conditions

The site may require mounting structures, cable protection, trenching, pipework, borehole access arrangements or other civil and electrical work. These requirements can affect the final quotation.

Do Solar Water Pumps Need Batteries?

Many solar water pumping systems can pump water during daylight without a battery bank. Water can instead be stored in a suitably sized tank for later use. This can be a practical arrangement where the water demand and pumping schedule allow it.

Batteries may be considered where the project requires electrical operation outside solar production hours or has other specific backup requirements. They add equipment, cost and maintenance considerations. The choice should be based on the actual operating requirement rather than assuming every solar pump needs batteries.

Benefits and Practical Considerations

  • Solar energy can reduce reliance on grid electricity or diesel-powered pumping.
  • Operating costs depend on the system design, maintenance and any backup energy used.
  • Water can be stored for use outside pumping hours.
  • Solar pumping can be useful in locations where grid access is limited.
  • Panels, pump components, cables and controls still require appropriate inspection and maintenance.
  • Water availability remains dependent on the capacity and condition of the source.

Solar Water Pumping for Farmers

For a farm, pump selection should start with the water requirement of the crops and the irrigation method. Drip irrigation, sprinklers and open-channel distribution have different flow and pressure requirements. The water source must also be able to provide the required volume without being over-pumped.

A useful assessment considers the area to be irrigated, crop water demand, irrigation schedule, source yield, delivery distance and storage arrangements. These details help determine whether the proposed pump and solar array are suitable for the intended use.

What to Prepare Before Requesting a Quotation

  • Your location and the intended use of the water.
  • The water source: borehole, well, river, dam, pond or existing tank.
  • Available borehole records, including depth and static or pumping water level, if known.
  • The required daily water volume or the number of users, animals or area to irrigate.
  • The height and approximate distance from the water source to the delivery point or tank.
  • Available pipe details, including diameter and approximate length, if already installed.
  • Any preference or requirement for grid, generator or battery backup.
  • Photographs, site information or an existing pump nameplate, where available.

If some measurements are not available, indicate what is known. The missing information can then be identified before a final pump and system specification is prepared.

Solar Water Pump Maintenance

Keep solar panels reasonably clean and check for shading, visible damage and loose mounting components. Cables, connectors, protective devices, the controller, pump and pipework should be inspected when servicing is required. Follow the equipment manufacturer's instructions and use a qualified person for electrical work.

If water output falls, possible causes include reduced sunlight, panel shading, a change in the water level, a blocked intake or pipe, a leak, a controller fault or a pump problem. Avoid running a pump outside its specified operating conditions. For a borehole, also consider whether the water source itself has changed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best solar water pump in Kenya?

The appropriate pump depends on the water source, required flow rate, total dynamic head, daily demand and the manufacturer's performance data. A pump that works well for one site may not be suitable for another.

Can a solar water pump work during cloudy weather?

A solar pump may continue operating when some sunlight is available, but reduced solar energy can reduce pump output or stop operation if the available power falls below the system's operating requirement. Actual behaviour depends on the pump and controller.

Does a solar water pump need a battery?

Not always. Many systems pump water during daylight and store it in a tank. Batteries are an option where the operating requirement calls for electrical energy outside solar production hours.

How long does a solar water pump last?

Service life varies by pump type, operating conditions, water quality, installation and maintenance. Check the manufacturer's stated specifications and warranty terms for the particular equipment being considered.
