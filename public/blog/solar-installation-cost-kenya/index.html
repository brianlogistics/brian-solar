Solar Installation Cost in Kenya (2026 Guide)

Planning a solar installation in Kenya starts with understanding your electricity needs, the type of system you require and the equipment needed to deliver reliable power. There is no single price that applies to every property: a small backup system for essential appliances is different from a complete off-grid installation for a home, business or farm.

This guide explains the main cost factors, the equipment involved and the information needed to prepare a suitable quotation.

What determines solar installation cost in Kenya?

1. Your electricity consumption

The first step is to identify the appliances you want to operate, their power ratings and how many hours they run each day. Daily energy use, measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh), helps determine the required solar panel capacity and battery storage.

Also consider appliances with high starting currents, such as refrigerators, pumps and some motors. Their starting demand can affect inverter selection even when their daily energy consumption is modest.

2. The type of solar system

  • Grid-tied: Works alongside the electricity grid. Battery backup is not normally included, so the system may not provide power during a grid outage unless designed with additional backup equipment.
  • Hybrid: Combines solar panels, an inverter, battery storage and grid supply. It can provide backup during outages when configured appropriately.
  • Off-grid: Designed to operate independently of the utility grid. Its design must account for energy demand, battery autonomy, available sunlight and periods of poor weather.

The equipment and installation requirements differ between these configurations, so compare quotations for the same system type and intended use.

3. Solar panels and mounting

The number and rating of panels depend on the energy requirement, local solar conditions, system losses and the time available to recharge batteries. Roof condition, orientation, shading and mounting structure can also affect installation work.

4. Inverter capacity and features

The inverter must support the expected running load and the starting demand of relevant appliances. Other considerations include battery compatibility, phase requirements, charging capability, monitoring features and provision for future expansion.

5. Battery capacity and chemistry

Battery requirements depend on the appliances that must remain powered when solar generation is unavailable, the required backup duration and the permitted depth of discharge. Lithium and lead-acid batteries have different costs, usable capacities, maintenance requirements and operating characteristics.

Compare usable energy and warranty conditions rather than comparing battery prices or nominal kWh figures alone.

6. Protection, wiring and installation work

A complete quotation should account for suitable DC and AC protection, isolators, breakers, surge protection where appropriate, correctly sized cables, earthing, connectors, mounting materials and installation labour. Site access, roof height, cable distances and electrical upgrades may affect the work involved.

Typical equipment in a solar installation

Solar panels

Convert sunlight into DC electricity for the system.

Solar inverter

Converts DC electricity into usable AC power and manages system operation according to its design.

Battery storage

Stores energy for use when solar generation is insufficient or unavailable.

Electrical protection

Helps protect equipment and wiring when correctly selected, installed and maintained.

How to estimate the size of your solar system

  1. List the appliances you want to power.
  2. Record each appliance's wattage and expected daily operating hours.
  3. Calculate the daily energy requirement in kWh.
  4. Identify the appliances that may run simultaneously and their starting demand.
  5. Choose the required backup duration and battery autonomy.
  6. Allow for system losses, local sunlight conditions and appropriate design margins.
  7. Have the final inverter, panel and battery selection checked against the actual site conditions.

Our solar sizing calculator can help you prepare an initial estimate. It is a planning tool, not a substitute for a professional site assessment and final system design.

Questions to ask before accepting a quotation

  • Does the quotation state the inverter make, model and rated capacity?
  • Does it list the panel quantity, wattage and manufacturer?
  • Does it state the battery chemistry, nominal capacity and usable capacity?
  • Are mounting, cabling, protection, earthing and labour included?
  • Are equipment warranties and workmanship terms clearly stated?
  • Does the design explain expected backup performance and its assumptions?
  • Are any exclusions, optional items or additional site works identified?

Why a site assessment matters

A reliable design considers more than the appliance list. Roof condition, shading, cable routes, existing electrical installations, equipment location, ventilation and future expansion can all influence the final specification. A site assessment helps identify these requirements before equipment is selected.

Frequently asked questions

How much does solar installation cost in Kenya?

The cost depends on system type, daily energy use, inverter capacity, panel quantity, battery storage, installation conditions and equipment specifications. A quotation based on your actual requirements is more useful than a generic package price.

Do I need batteries for my solar system?

Not every system requires batteries. Grid-tied systems can operate without battery storage, while hybrid and off-grid systems commonly include batteries when backup or energy storage is required.

Can I expand my solar system later?

Expansion may be possible, but it depends on inverter capacity, battery compatibility, panel input limits, protection equipment and the original system design. Discuss future loads before installation.

How long will a solar battery provide backup?

Backup time depends on usable battery energy and the total load being supplied. Higher loads use stored energy more quickly. A design should state its assumed load and backup duration.

Does a solar installation eliminate my electricity bill?

Not necessarily. Results depend on system configuration, energy consumption, solar production, grid usage and applicable utility arrangements. Avoid treating a fixed percentage of bill reduction as guaranteed.
