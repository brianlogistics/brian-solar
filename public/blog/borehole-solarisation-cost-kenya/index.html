Borehole Solarisation Cost in Kenya (2026 Guide)

Solarising a borehole means using solar electricity to operate a water pump. It can reduce reliance on grid electricity or fuel, particularly where water must be pumped regularly. However, the cost and performance of a borehole solar system depend on the water requirement, pumping depth, site conditions and the equipment selected.

This guide explains the main components, sizing considerations and installation factors to assess before requesting a quotation in Kenya.

What determines borehole solarisation cost?

1. Borehole depth and pumping level

The total drilled depth alone does not determine the pump requirement. The static water level and, more importantly, the dynamic water level while pumping help establish the lift the pump must overcome. A borehole that experiences a significant water-level drop during pumping may need a different pump selection from one with a stable level.

2. Total dynamic head

Total dynamic head is the overall pressure head the pump must overcome. It includes the lift from the pumping water level to the delivery point, pressure needed at the outlet and losses through pipes, bends, valves and fittings.

Incorrect head estimates can result in a pump that delivers too little water or operates outside its intended performance range.

3. Daily water demand and flow rate

The system should be designed around the volume of water required each day and when that water is needed. Domestic use, livestock, irrigation and commercial applications can have very different demand patterns.

Daily volume and the required delivery time help determine the flow rate. The selected pump must be able to deliver that flow at the calculated head, subject to the borehole's sustainable yield.

4. Pump type, power and controller

The pump must match the borehole, water conditions, required flow and total dynamic head. Its performance curve should be checked at the intended operating point. The solar pump controller must also be compatible with the pump motor and the selected solar array.

5. Solar panels and mounting structure

The solar array is sized to provide the energy required by the pump under the site's solar conditions. Panel quantity and rating depend on pump power, operating hours, controller requirements, system losses and the desired daily water output.

6. Pipes, cabling and protection

Pipe diameter and length influence friction losses. Electrical cable size must account for current, cable distance and voltage drop. The installation may also require suitable isolators, protective devices, earthing, connectors, mounting materials and protection against environmental exposure.

7. Water storage and distribution

A storage tank can allow water to be pumped during available sunlight and used later. Tank capacity, elevation, distribution pipework, pressure requirements and any additional booster pump should be considered as part of the complete water system.

Main components of a solar borehole system

Submersible pump

Lifts water from the borehole at the flow and head required by the application.

Solar pump controller

Controls the pump and manages the electrical input according to system compatibility.

Solar panel array

Supplies electricity to operate the pump during available sunlight.

Tank and pipework

Stores and delivers pumped water to the required points of use.

Information needed to size a borehole solar pump

A professional assessment should establish the following information before equipment is selected:

  • Borehole depth and available borehole completion records.
  • Static and dynamic water levels, where measured.
  • Borehole yield and any recommended pumping limits.
  • Required daily water volume and preferred pumping schedule.
  • Delivery height, tank location and required outlet pressure.
  • Pipe material, diameter, length and route.
  • Water quality and any sand or sediment concerns.
  • Available space and conditions for mounting solar panels.
  • Whether grid or generator backup is required.

Why pump sizing must use the pump curve

A pump's advertised power rating does not by itself tell you how much water it will deliver. Flow changes with pumping head, and each pump has a performance curve showing its expected operating range. The selected pump should meet the required flow at the calculated total dynamic head without exceeding the borehole's sustainable yield.

The solar array and controller must then be matched to the selected pump and the site's expected solar conditions. Oversizing or undersizing individual components without checking compatibility can affect cost, reliability and water output.

Solar borehole pumping versus grid or generator pumping

Solar pumping uses energy from solar panels and is most productive when sunlight is available. Grid-powered pumping depends on the electricity supply and applicable tariffs. Generator pumping depends on fuel availability, fuel cost and maintenance.

The most suitable arrangement depends on the site's existing infrastructure, water demand, operating schedule and lifetime operating costs. Solar pumping does not automatically eliminate all operating expenses: cleaning, inspections, pump servicing and repairs still matter.

Installation and maintenance considerations

  • Confirm the borehole condition and pump installation requirements before lowering equipment.
  • Install the pump at the depth recommended for the borehole and pump design.
  • Use suitable cable, pipework, joints and electrical protection.
  • Secure panels on a stable structure with suitable orientation and minimal shading.
  • Protect the controller and electrical connections from water and excessive heat.
  • Inspect panels, mounting, cables, controller readings and water output periodically.
  • Investigate changes in flow, unusual noise, frequent trips or excessive sediment promptly.

Why a site assessment is necessary

Two boreholes with similar depths may require different systems because their water levels, yields, delivery heights, pipework and daily demand differ. A site assessment and accurate pumping information help prevent unsuitable pump selection and unrealistic cost estimates.

Frequently asked questions

Can solar panels run a borehole pump directly?

A compatible solar pump controller is commonly used to manage power from the solar array and operate the pump. The controller and panels must be matched to the pump's electrical requirements.

Do I need batteries for solar borehole pumping?

Many solar water-pumping systems operate during daylight without batteries. Water can be stored in a tank for later use. Batteries may be considered for specific operating requirements, but they add cost and maintenance considerations.

How many solar panels are needed for a borehole pump?

The number depends on pump specifications, required water output, total dynamic head, panel rating, controller limits and local solar conditions. It should be determined from the pump and system design rather than pump horsepower alone.

Can a solar pump supply water for irrigation?

Yes, if the borehole yield, pump performance, daily water requirement, storage and irrigation distribution system are properly assessed. Irrigation demand can vary significantly by crop, area and watering method.

Will solar pumping work on cloudy days?

Solar output decreases when sunlight is reduced, which can lower pumping performance. A suitable design considers local solar conditions and may include water storage or an alternative backup arrangement where required.

Does solarisation guarantee lower pumping costs?

It can reduce dependence on grid electricity or generator fuel, but actual savings depend on the existing energy source, water demand, system cost, maintenance and operating conditions. No fixed savings percentage should be assumed without a project-specific assessment.
