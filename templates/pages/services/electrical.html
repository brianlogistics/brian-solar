Electrical Work for New Builds, Renovations and Existing Homes

If you are building a house, renovating rooms or moving into a property that needs electrical attention, it helps to plan the electrical work around how the property will actually be used. Consider lighting, sockets, kitchen appliances, water pumps, water heaters, security equipment and future additions.

For homeowners, discussing these requirements early can help establish the scope of wiring, electrical points and protection equipment before work begins. For businesses, farms and institutions, the assessment should also consider the equipment and activities the installation needs to support.

Electrical Problems That Need Attention

Frequent circuit tripping, burning smells, unusually warm sockets, damaged switches, exposed wiring or flickering lights can indicate an electrical problem. Stop using affected equipment where it is unsafe to do so and arrange an assessment by a suitably qualified professional. Do not attempt to investigate live wiring yourself.

What Affects the Cost of Electrical Work?

Electrical quotations depend on the actual work required. Important factors can include:

  • The property size, layout and type of building.
  • The number and location of lighting points, sockets and circuits.
  • Whether the work is for a new installation, renovation or repair.
  • The condition of existing wiring and distribution equipment.
  • Cable routes, access to work areas and installation complexity.
  • The equipment, protection components and materials required.
  • Testing requirements and the agreed scope of work.

Share your project details and, where possible, photographs or drawings so we can understand the work you have in mind. A site assessment may be needed before the scope and quotation can be confirmed.
